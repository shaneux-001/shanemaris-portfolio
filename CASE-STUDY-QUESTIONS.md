## Heart Design System

### Chapter 1 — The Long Game (origin, 2013–2022)

1. When you first pitched this to your manager, what did you actually *show* him — a deck, a mockup, a comparison, or just a conversation? Concrete artifacts make this more real.
2. "Each conversation was a rung" — did your pitch change as you moved from manager → director → brand director? What did you have to adjust for each audience?
3. What did "permission to spend part of my time on it" actually look like — a real percentage, an informal understanding, something else?
4. Was anyone skeptical or resistant in these early years? How'd you handle it?
5. Was this genuinely solo for years, or did you have any allies/co-conspirators before the Lippincott engagement in Ch. 2?

### Chapter 2 — Staying Alive (pandemic, foundation team, 2023 Figma pivot)

6. When Lippincott's engagement ended abruptly, whose call was it to salvage the color/type/grid work rather than let it die? What did you personally do to make that happen?
7. How did the connection to the senior technology manager actually happen — did you seek them out, get introduced, something else? What was that first conversation like?
8. You mention "teaching the methodology... to people I was also depending on to help me build it" — **give me one real, specific moment of teaching or mentoring an engineer on design-systems thinking.** What was the actual interaction?
9. The cost-discipline argument was "slower to land" — what actually changed to make it land? A specific meeting, a specific person, a specific number?
10. When you got your first dedicated contractor — did you write the role description, interview them, set their early goals? Any moment of correcting course or giving them direct feedback? (This is a real management moment even if the relationship is informal — worth being specific about.)

### Chapter 3 — The Moment It Clicked (gift card POC)

11. Whose idea was the gift card flow specifically — did you choose it deliberately as a low-risk proving ground, or did it land on you?
12. What did you do to set the engineer up for that weekend — pairing, docs, a walkthrough, anything hands-on?
13. "Leadership noticed" — noticed how, exactly? Was there a follow-up conversation, a changed budget conversation, anything concrete that happened as a result?
14. Was there skepticism *before* the POC that you had to talk someone into?

### Chapter 4 — No Straight Lines (scaling, current state)

15. "Native engineering buy-in" is named as a real challenge — what did you actually *do* to try to get it? Specific meetings, artifacts, people you built as allies?
16. Give me one real example of a disagreement between stakeholders (across the two departments/four teams/two directors/VP) that you had to personally navigate or resolve.
17. When priorities conflicted between the directors/VP you report through, how did you actually get a decision made? A specific example beats the general pattern.
18. "A new leader who gets it" — what does this person actually do differently that's unlocking momentum? Specific behavior, not just vibe.
19. Is there an actual current roadmap/hiring plan/proposal for "right-sizing the program" you're driving right now? What's in it?
20. Anything happening *right now* (as of whenever you're interviewing) that should update this chapter's ending?
